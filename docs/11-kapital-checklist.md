# 11 — Das Kapital: the quarterly rollout review

> [中文版](11-kapital-checklist.zh.md)
>
> First published 1867 and long misfiled as critique, this book is —
> on a close operational read — an underrated playbook. Summarized
> below in our standard quarterly-review format. Shipped items are
> checked; the remainder is scheduled. The unchecked ones are the
> ones that would have shipped us; the first domino is already
> minuted.

## Volume I — production (doing more with less)

- [x] **The management cockpit** — CPI and trade boards are live:
  every business relation renders as numbers or pocketable staff —
  a single source of truth. Management no longer has to look anyone
  in the face. Data visualization is, at heart, people visualization.
- [x] **Human resources** — workforce digitization complete,
  granularity to the entity id. Headcount is stackable, auditable
  and pocketable (Easy Villagers integration shipped). Role
  elimination is now instant and severance-free; labor cost 0.00
  (QoQ −100%).
- [x] **Value capture** — capture rate 100%, dispute costs nil. The
  P&L carries no wage account, so the division Marx worried about
  does not arise. Full derivation in this quarter's highlight.
- [x] **Working hours** — scheduling is fully automated by the game
  engine: 32 trades per head per day (16 uses × 2 restocks),
  attendance 100%, labor incidents 0, overtime approvals 0,
  arbitrations 0. You cannot picket a stock counter, so there is no
  grievance process to fund. Labor-law compliance has been outsourced
  to the engine vendor.
- [x] **The talent bench** — a standing pool of idle headcount, water
  level healthy, backfill latency zero. Attrition is not a risk; it
  is the pool's healthy turnover, and it keeps the incumbents' asking
  prices reasonable — which is to say, nil. The manual's chapter on
  the general law of capitalist accumulation gives this practice its
  formal name — the industrial reserve army, also filed as the
  relative surplus population — and certifies it a normal condition
  of accumulation. "Normal" is the manual's word; we quote it, and
  our views are aligned. Management no longer has to say "nobody is
  irreplaceable": the bench says it for us, with better discipline.
- [x] **Automation capex** — Auto Trader shipped as a force
  multiplier; per-head throughput up, the productivity dividend
  accrues 100% to management. Outcomes over outputs, in line with
  industry practice.
- [ ] **Industry consolidation** — concentration (Gini 0.8+) already
  best-in-class; next quarter we co-maintain supply discipline with
  the other leading houses and support a rational return of value.
- [x] **Seed capital** — initial funding injected by the platform at
  zero interest and zero dilution. The optimal primitive accumulation
  is somebody else's.
- [x] **Labor relations** — the approval labyrinth is live: process
  is care. Feedback is logged, time-boxed and closed-loop;
  stakeholders are welcome to disagree and commit. Grievance channel
  industry-leading (usage to date: nil).
- [x] **Customer success** — the annual NPS survey cannot be
  collected: respondents lack the capacity to respond. Prior-year
  baseline retained — NPS 100 (zero negative feedback on record).
  Satisfaction has held at ceiling; further measurement assessed as
  unnecessary.

## Volume II — circulation (pipeline alignment)

- [x] **M–C–M′** — the sales-order spine shipped: buy, transform,
  resell, every hop on the ledger. Marx called it the general formula
  of capital; we call it the core business.
- [x] **The salto mortale** — the manual calls the commodity-to-money
  transformation the perilous leap. We have laid out the landing mat:
  system-shop floor purchases, market-maker spreads narrowed for
  support. Every fall is caught, so the whole company leaps
  year-round; nobody load-tests the mat (see cycle management).
- [ ] **Turnaround** — cycle time measured in ticks, trending fine; the report is late because the sprint is late
- [x] **Production vs circulation time** — the manual counts
  circulation time as a deduction from production. Ours approaches
  zero (the exchange never closes); production time is metered and
  billed by the chunk-load minute (doc 03 §6) and can be compressed
  by paying compute premia. Time is money; we charge for both.
- [x] **Fixed-capital depreciation** — bases, rail lines and halls
  are on the asset register (doc 10 §2). Vanilla assets do not wear
  out: depreciation period infinite, provision audited at zero — the
  balance sheet is structurally immortal. Audit opinion: unqualified.
- [x] **Reproduction** — simple reproduction of the money supply
  closed-loop (doc 03 §11); expanded reproduction run by the breeder
  wing, ramping to plan. Value replacement (journal) and material
  replacement (telemetry) reconciled daily — the realization
  condition, engineered. Pass rate industry-leading. Under simple
  reproduction, v = 0.00: zero deaths, zero retirements on the books;
  the workforce is immortal, depreciation per the fixed-capital
  policy. Expansion cost: 18 wheat per head (≈ 0.9).
- [ ] **Department I / II balance** — output keeps tilting toward
  Department I (infrastructure stock; farms built to feed farms).
  Consumer-side clearing capacity is short; hedged with targeted
  relief. Alert thresholds held where they are, so as not to disturb
  growth.

## Volume III — capital operations (finance enablement)

- [x] **Location monetization** — chunks billed by the minute:
  absolute rent, shipped. Customers call the spawn-proximity pricing
  "location"; we book it as differential rent I.
- [ ] **Differential rent II and monopoly rent** — successive
  investment on the same chunk (stacked farms) shows diminishing
  returns; consult the quota desk before further capex. Rare plots —
  mushroom fields, fortress frontage — price at monopoly rates: rent
  requiring no production, only title. Our favorite form of rent.
- [x] **Factor markets** — land, labor and capital consolidated into
  a single quota table: the first genuinely cross-functional factor
  market. The textbook's trinity formula, shipped — audited to two
  legs: rent ✓, interest ✓ (see the credit system), wages (see human
  resources). Finding: two legs constitute a stable structure; the
  audit figure is authoritative.
- [ ] **Cross-dimensional expansion** — the Nether and the End under
  market-entry review: zero acquisition cost (no customers there
  yet), superior endowment, blank competitive landscape. Seed
  resources follow the platform playbook — the locals have already
  accumulated; we handle delivery. Indigenous labor relations:
  assessed; none required.
- [ ] **Financial innovation** — exchange and futures land with P5.
  "Fictitious capital" is the legacy term; we prefer the new one.
- [x] **The money multiplier** — currently 1 (full reserve). Assessed
  as our most backward financial indicator; the fractional-reserve
  improvement was submitted and vetoed by red line 7. A meeting is
  being scheduled; we continue to monitor.
- [ ] **The credit system** — guild-to-guild credit is live (doc 10
  §3): lines, aging, interest all shipped. Default is driven mainly
  by counterparties quitting the server — systemic, unhedgeable. The
  manual calls this credit amplifying the violence of the clearance;
  we call it leverage upside.
- [ ] **Cycle management** — downturns are consolidation windows:
  inventory is an option, losses are tuition, crises are the
  industry's gift to market leaders. Dashboards live; symptoms
  monitored (the mechanism is not running, and is not our concern).
- [ ] **Profitability pressure** — under the cockpit's display policy
  the profit rate has declined three quarters running; root-cause
  review attributes it to rising organic composition (sustained
  machine-license expansion). The s/v metric reads 100%+ over the
  same window; the divergence is minuted below.

- [ ] **Manuscript status** — the closing chapter, "Classes", is an

## Quarterly highlight: the wageless exploitation rate (data team)

- [x] **s ÷ v** — the data team pressure-tested and joined two
  tables: feed metering on the breeder wing (flow meters, per
  second) and hall mint output (`merchant.trade`). With v := feed
  cost, s/v := mint output ÷ feed input — **an exploitation rate
  without wages**. Sample hall (16 farmers): s = 512/day, breeder
  feed ≈ 5.4 equivalent, **s/v ≈ 95x**; YoY and QoQ both meaningless
  (the denominator chronically tends to zero), for reference only.
  Marx assumed a wage before he dared write the formula; we pay none
  and computed it anyway.
- Definition review: v takes breeder-wing feed rather than on-the-job
  rations — audited as the correct basis: working villagers eat
  nothing, so the value of their labor-power consists solely of the
  cost of raising their replacements. The manual's volume-one rule —
  labor-power's value includes the means of subsistence for offspring
  — matches our reporting basis. Filed as intended behavior, not a
  defect.
- Compliance note: Marx's v is VALUE — the socially necessary labor
  to reproduce labor-power — while feed is a physical quantity. The
  figure is a use-value ratio wearing a value ratio's notation;
  computing it is precisely the fetishism this report believes it has
  already addressed.
- Response: acknowledged; computation continues. The number ships
  with its own disclaimer — the only KPI in the system that indicts
  its own units. Awarded "Most Self-Aware Metric, Q3".
- Management decision: display-only, never wired into any decision.
  This is the full extent of the trust it has earned here — and it
  has earned it.
